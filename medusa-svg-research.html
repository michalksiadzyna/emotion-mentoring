Historical Note: The Rondanini Medusa

The Medusa Rondanini is a Roman copy of a 5th century BC classical Greek original, attributed to a sculptor from the age of Phidias. It resides in the Glyptothek in Munich, Germany. Unlike earlier grotesque gorgon depictions, the Rondanini Medusa is rendered as beautiful and humanized -- a serene, idealized feminine face with gracefully coiling snake hair. This is the sculpture that inspired the Versace Medusa logo and represents the pinnacle of "beautiful Medusa" aesthetic in art history.

The evolution of Medusa in art: Archaic period (8th-6th c. BC) -- grotesque demon with tusks and tongue. Classical period (5th-4th c. BC) -- humanized, beautiful face (Rondanini type). Hellenistic to Roman -- even more beautiful, often tragic. Neoclassical (Canova, etc.) -- based directly on the Rondanini marble.
