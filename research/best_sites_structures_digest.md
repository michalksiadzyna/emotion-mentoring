# Best-in-Class Site Structures Digest — High-Trust, High-Ticket Personal Services

> Research satellite (2026-07-11). Linked to: OPERATION CHAMPAGNE structure decision.
> 13 sites dissected: elite executive coaches, private-client luxury services, award one-pagers, conversion canon.

## Category 1 — Elite executive coaches

- **Marshall Goldsmith** (marshallgoldsmith.com): hero is literally his name + mission sentence. Apex-predator pattern — reputation precedes, site sells nothing, no visible "hire me". 16 named heavyweight testimonials. Zero pricing.
- **Reboot.io / Jerry Colonna**: worldview hero ("Better Humans Make Better Leaders") before any service description. CEO testimonial as section 2 (Brad Feld: "the best CEO coach I've ever met"). All CTAs route to ONE inquiry anchor. Coaching page ends in a deliberately high-friction form (what prompted you, what do you hope to achieve, how do you learn) — **the form IS the first session**. "Rates vary" — no numbers. Engagement framed as two sessions/month, month-to-month.
- **Founder Coach / Dave Bailey**: "CEO coaching for VC-backed scale-ups" — precise audience naming. Free 5-min diagnostic as low-friction side door (repeated 3×). Testimonials tagged with funding amounts ($35M–$501M) for self-recognition. Trademarked proprietary system names. No pricing.
- **Alisa Cohn**: newsletter hero — cautionary contrast; audience-building dilutes private-client acquisition.
- **CEO Coaching International**: quantified-outcome positioning ("107 exits worth $26.6B", NPS 64.91), ~17 CTAs — volume model, wrong register for a 12-client cap.
- **Vistage**: "Don't let isolation stall you" — loneliness named as the enemy in the subhead. Qualification as honor ("Chairs carefully select members", $5M+ companies). Survival stats as proof (+5.8% in 2008 vs −9.2% non-members).

## Category 2 — Luxury / private-client services

- **Sollis Health** (concierge medicine): exclusivity via access and speed, never opulence. "Members Only" direct lines. Pricing withheld ("personalized quote"). Membership framing.
- **Private Medical** (UHNW concierge medicine): **the closest structural analog**. CTA is a single word — "Inquire" — nav, hero, footer. Member stories use titles only, no names. Zero pricing; the inquiry form gauges budget. Capacity published ("1,000 families"), price hidden.
- **J.P. Morgan Private Bank**: insights-led homepage; "You've built something extraordinary" second-person aspirational address; trust by demonstrating intelligence, not claiming it.
- **Aman Resorts**: luxury through subtraction — ~80 words before first CTA, one image + one idea + one soft CTA per section, "Discover more" as softest verb.
- **Clare Locke LLP** (elite crisis law): opens with a client quote before saying one word about itself. "Some of our biggest victories are the flawed stories that never get published" — sells discretion by describing invisible wins. Copy as documentation, not persuasion. Monochrome.

## Category 3 — Award-grade one-pagers

- Elliott Mangham (Awwwards SOTD): single page, TWO colors total, GSAP scroll reveals, preloader; performance scored as heavily as design.
- Recurring winner traits: typography-driven, restrained palettes, 5–8 sections for personal brands.
- Scrollytelling rules: pinned sections, pacing that breathes, "restraint over spectacle — not every section needs animation", motion language matched to the subject's gravity.
- **Conversion moment = narrative resolution**: placed at the end of the scroll story, never interrupting it.

## Category 4 — Conversion canon (Julian Shapiro, CXL, Thrive teardowns)

- Canonical order: hero → social proof immediately after → CTA → objection handling → repeat CTA.
- "Don't fear writing a long homepage. But ensure your hero is incredible."
- High-ticket teardowns: "Who This Is For / NOT For" pair builds trust; testimonials woven at the point of the claim they support; consultation framed as transformation, not sales call.

## STRUCTURAL PATTERNS (12)

1. **Worldview hero, not service hero** — lead with a belief or a named enemy, never "I offer services."
2. **Proof immediately after hero, third-party voiced** — elite sites never praise themselves; clients/press do.
3. **One CTA verb, repeated, never varied** — "Inquire"-class soft verb, 3–4 placements.
4. **The inquiry form IS the first session** — thoughtful friction filters and demonstrates quality of attention.
5. **Pricing opacity + capacity transparency** — hide the price, publish the cap ("12 private engagements" fits exactly).
6. **Explicit qualification as flattery** — name precisely who you serve; the client applies, the mentor never sells.
7. **Confidentiality shown, never claimed** — anonymized-but-specific testimonials ("CEO, fintech, 140 employees") beat "100% confidential" bullets.
8. **Numbers with denominators** — verifiable, specific figures with context; vague claims mark weak sites.
9. **Luxury = subtraction** — emptiness reads expensive; density reads mass-market.
10. **7±2 sections, conversion as resolution** — hero → proof → method → qualification → objections → capacity → ask → quiet footer.
11. **Restrained motion, tonally matched** — slow fade-ups, weighty scroll, fast load.
12. **One low-friction side door maximum** — an essay/letter/diagnostic for the not-yet-ready; more dilutes.

## ANTI-PATTERNS (10)

1. Visible selling pressure (countdowns, urgency banners, exit popups) — absent from every elite site.
2. Calendar-first booking — instant bookability signals availability, which signals non-exclusivity. Inquiry step first.
3. Price anchoring / discount framing.
4. Self-praise adjectives in first person ("world-class", "elite") — superlatives live only inside quoted testimonials.
5. Newsletter hero on a client-acquisition page.
6. Testimonial dumps at page bottom.
7. Deliverables menus ("12 sessions, email support, worksheets") — mid-market packaging.
8. Stock photography, busy palettes, multiple CTA styles.
9. Animation spectacle (scroll-hijacking, per-section animation) — juries and buyers both punish it.
10. Explaining coaching generically / leading with industry stats — assume a sophisticated reader, speak to their situation immediately.
