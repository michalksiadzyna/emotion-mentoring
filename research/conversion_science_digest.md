# Conversion Science Digest — Evidence Base for the Rebuild Structure

> Research satellite (2026-07-11). Linked to: OPERATION CHAMPAGNE structure decision.
> Legend: [STRONG] = real study/experiment/large dataset. [MODERATE] = documented single case study or vendor benchmark. [OPINION] = practitioner guidance without published data.

## 1. Section count & order / page length

- [STRONG] Unbounce Conversion Benchmark Report (44,000+ landing pages, 33M+ conversions): shorter, simpler copy correlates with higher conversion; **-24.3% correlation between difficult words (3+ syllables) and conversion rate**. Professional services median ~6.1% at modest word counts. Correlational, mostly low/mid-ticket. https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/
- [STRONG] CXL "Long Form or Short Form?": expensive/rational/intimidating purchases → **longer copy outperforms** (Crazy Egg ~20x longer page +30%, Conversion Rate Experts). Short wins for cheap/impulse. Length must match decision complexity and risk. https://cxl.com/blog/long-form-or-short-form/
- [MODERATE] No credible study prescribes an exact section count. Supported instead: **sequence logic** — MECLABS "thought sequence" (Where am I? What can I do? Why do it? Why from you? What does it cost?), value before ask. MarketingSherpa value-sequencing teardown: **+638% conversions**. https://marketingexperiments.com/conversion-marketing/lpo-eyepath-thought-conversion ; https://marketingsherpa.com/article/how-to/sequence-value-on-a-landing-page-example
- [STRONG] NNGroup scrolling eyetracking (2018): **57% of viewing time above the fold; 74% in first two screenfuls**. Order sections strictly by persuasive priority. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/
- Verdict: long-ish page, front-loaded value, every section earning its position, plain words.

## 2. Form fields

- [MODERATE] HubSpot (40,000+ pages): **3-field forms highest (~25%)**; decline as fields increase. https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/form-design
- [MODERATE] Imagescape: 11 → 4 fields → **+120% conversions**.
- [MODERATE] Expedia: removing one ambiguous field ("Company name") recovered **~$12M/year**. Every field must have zero ambiguity.
- [STRONG-ish counter] CXL "Should You Really Reduce Form Fields?": blind cutting is cargo-cult; qualifying fields can stay. MECLABS: one field made optional → **+275%**; phone to step 2 → **+68%**; cut fields → **+96% leads**. https://cxl.com/blog/reduce-form-fields/
- [MODERATE] MarketingExperiments: lower conversion rate is legitimate when the form filters intent — fewer, better-qualified leads. Fits a 12-client practice.
- Verdict: **2–4 fields** (name, email, optionally one qualifying question). Phone optional or deferred.

## 3. Single CTA

- [STRONG] Unbounce attention ratio: **1 link = 13.5% avg conversion vs 11.9% (2–4 links) vs 10.5% (5+)**. Ideal ratio 1:1. https://unbounce.com/conversion-glossary/definition/attention-ratio/
- [MODERATE] Whirlpool/MarketingSherpa: 4 CTAs → 1 → **+42% clickthrough**.
- [OPINION] Repeating the SAME CTA at intervals is not "multiple CTAs" — hero + mid + end placement of the identical action is standard (Unbounce, KlientBoost).
- Verdict: one action ("book the conversation"), repeated verbatim 3–4×; no nav menu, no link farm.

## 4. Above the fold / hook

- [STRONG] Lindgaard et al. 2006: visual appeal judgments form within **50 ms**, halo effect on credibility.
- [STRONG] Google 2012 (Tuch/Presslaber): **low visual complexity + high prototypicality** drive 50 ms trust. Calm, recognizably "serious professional" wins. https://research.google.com/pubs/archive/38315.pdf
- [STRONG] NNGroup: **79% scan, 16% read**; F-pattern — first line and left-anchored openers carry the weight.
- [STRONG] MECLABS "clarity trumps persuasion": clarity over cleverness → **+200% lift** documented. https://marketingexperiments.com/copywriting/web-messaging-clarity-conversion
- [STRONG] Meta-analysis of **8,977 headline experiments** (Nature Sci Reports 2024): concrete beats vague; pure curiosity-gap mixed-to-negative; vagueness costs trust. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-81575-9
- [MODERATE] Copyhackers: cleverness ON TOP of clarity can win (+18%); cleverness INSTEAD of clarity loses.
- Verdict: hero instantly clear what/for whom; restrained intrigue in the subline at most.

## 5. Social proof placement & format

- [STRONG] Spiegel Research Center: reviews raise purchase probability up to **270%**; effect LARGER for expensive products (**+380% high-price vs +190% low-price**); trust peaks at **4.2–4.5 stars** — perfection reads fake; 82% seek negative signals. https://spiegel.medill.northwestern.edu/how-online-reviews-influence-sales/
- [STRONG] BrightLocal annual survey: trust in generic reviews declining; recency matters.
- [MODERATE] Testimonial adjacent to CTA lifts CTR **15–40%** — proof at the moment of hesitation beats a proof ghetto.
- [OPINION grounded in NN/g] Unattributed testimonials read fabricated. Confidential-service substitute: verifiable specificity ("CEO, industrial manufacturing, 240 employees") + explicit anonymity policy framed as proof of discretion.
- Verdict: distribute proof — one specific testimonial next to each CTA instance; attribute as far as confidentiality allows; state the policy.

## 6. Psychology frameworks

- [STRONG] MECLABS Conversion Sequence Heuristic **C = 4m + 3v + 2(i−f) − 2a** (10,000+ experiments). For €3K+/month confidential service, **anxiety is the dominant lever** — confidentiality guarantees, what-happens-next transparency, no-obligation framing carry double weight. https://meclabs.com/about/heuristic
- [STRONG mechanism / OPINION packaging] PAS & StoryBrand rest on loss aversion (Kahneman & Tversky 1979, losses ≈ 2-3× gains); the frameworks themselves lack independent validation.
- [STRONG] Scarcity meta-analysis (Barton et al. 2022, 131 studies): scarcity works; **limited-quantity beats limited-time** for uniqueness-seeking buyers (CEOs). https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.890350/full
- [STRONG warning] NNGroup: manufactured urgency (countdowns, popups) destroys trust with sophisticated users. **Authentic quantity scarcity stated calmly** ("12 private engagements") is the evidence-compatible form. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scarcity-principle-ux/
- Verdict: MECLABS sequence as page spine; PAS rhythm inside problem section only; real scarcity stated once; authority via specifics not badges.

## 7. Animation & conversion

- [STRONG] NNGroup: splash/intro screens among the most rejected patterns — users hunt for "skip", dismiss intros even when informative. "Flash: 99% Bad" (Nielsen 2000). https://www.nngroup.com/articles/making-hated-design-elements-work/
- [STRONG] Google/Deloitte "Milliseconds Make Millions" (37 brands): **0.1s mobile speed improvement → +8.3% lead-gen form submissions**. Any intro delaying interactive content is measurable loss. https://www.deloitte.com/ie/en/services/consulting/research/milliseconds-make-millions.html
- [MODERATE] Bouncing payment button → **−11% conversions** (read as buggy). NNGroup: purposeful UI animation 100–500 ms.
- [OPINION/standard] Reconciliation: intro plays **once per session (sessionStorage)**, visible skip, never blocks CTA render; content painted underneath.
- Verdict: no gating intro. Signature reveal ≤ ~1.5s, once, skippable, hero interactive from first paint.

## Design implications — 15 hard rules

1. One action only; attention ratio 1:1 (13.5% vs 10.5%).
2. Long page, front-loaded: value prop + who-it's-for + first CTA within screens 1–2 (57%/74% attention data).
3. Hero passes the 5-second clarity test; clarity before intrigue (+200% MECLABS; 8,977-headline meta).
4. Visually calm, recognizably professional — restraint IS the premium signal (50 ms studies).
5. Identical CTA repeated 3–4× (hero, after proof, after process, end).
6. CTA language reduces anxiety: "confidential", "20–30 minutes", what happens next. Anxiety = −2 coefficient.
7. Form 2–4 fields: name + email + one qualifying question. Phone optional/omitted.
8. A specific testimonial beside every CTA instance (+15–40% CTR; proof works harder for expensive: +380%).
9. Attribute proof as far as confidentiality allows; state anonymity policy as evidence of discretion.
10. Imperfection is credible: one honest "who this is NOT for" section (trust peaks below perfection).
11. Scarcity: real number stated once, calmly. Zero countdowns/theatrics.
12. Page spine = MECLABS: motivation-match → value prop → friction removal → anxiety dissolution → ask. Never agitate past a CEO's dignity.
13. No blocking intro (0.1s ≈ −8.3% lead-gen). Signature reveal ≤1.5s, once per session, skippable, content underneath.
14. Micro-animation 100–500 ms, purposeful; never animate the CTA in ways reading as instability (−11%).
15. Plain words; short sentences; concrete nouns; front-loaded openers (F-pattern; −24.3% difficult-word penalty).

Confidence: rules 1, 3, 4, 8, 11, 13, 15 rest on strong published data; the rest combine strong underlying research with documented cases or reasoned adaptation.
