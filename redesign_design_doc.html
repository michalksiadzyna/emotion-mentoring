Mission

1. The Thesis

The client: the CEO. One audience, named in the first headline. No second audience appears anywhere — every widening word ("leaders", "executives", "high achievers") refunds the status the word CEO bought. This is a verified pattern failure on competitor sites.

The product: optimization of the CEO's mind. Every company decision runs through one mind; that mind carries more load than it was tuned for — responsibilities, pressure, unhandled emotional weight. The service restores and upgrades its function under load: better algorithms for decisions, problems, relationships, life.

The market gap (verified): the CEO-coaching segment speaks two dialects — the pressure dialect ("overwhelm, you don't have to do it alone") and Reboot's depth dialect ("radical self-inquiry"). The middle lane — a precise, mechanistic account of the CEO's mind under load, sold privately to the individual CEO — is empty. Tignum sells "mental agility under high load" to Fortune-500 companies as corporate contracts; nobody sells it to the person in the chair at €3,000+/month. That lane is ours.

The Trojan horse (documented): in HBR's coach survey, 3% of coaches are hired for personal issues; 76% end up working on them. CEOs buy performance. The work turns out to be inner work. We sell the former and deliver both — which is exactly the Shen Method's nature.

BCEO pain — verified statistics (honest ammunition for the fear engine)

Every number traces to a named study; the popular "CEOs make 35,000 decisions a day" line is folklore and stays out:

1. Half of CEOs report loneliness in the role; 61% say it hinders their performance (RHR International survey, via Harvard Business Review).
2. Nearly two-thirds of CEOs receive no outside advice on their leadership — nearly all say they want it (Stanford GSB / Miles Group, 200+ CEOs).
3. ~70% of C-suite executives have seriously considered quitting for their well-being (Deloitte × Workplace Intelligence, 2,100 respondents).
4. 85% of leaders suffered "decision distress" — regret, guilt, second-guessing — in the past year; 59% face a decision dilemma daily or more (Oracle, 14,000+ respondents).
5. CEOs average 62.5 hours/week, working 79% of weekend days and 70% of holidays (Harvard Business School, 60,000 logged hours).
6. 43% of executives don't get enough sleep at least four nights a week (McKinsey); 90% of founder-CEOs lose sleep over the possibility of failure (Norwest CEO Journey Study).

These are the icebergs, documented. The fear engine quotes them; nothing is invented.

CSegment behavior — the CEO teardown patterns

1. Price invisible, 9 of 9 sites. The segment treats disclosure as down-market. (Our ruling on this is open — see Decisions.)
2. "CEO" in the first headline on every strong site; qualification by company scale ("$50M–$2B enterprises"), a status ladder visitors self-place on.
3. Entry is a conversation, never a checkout. Best mechanics found: Glenn Gow's "See If We're a Fit" — books like a calendar link, reads like mutual selection.
4. Proof is peer-status proof: famous client names, hard business metrics, or testimonial mass from sitting CEOs. Coaching credentials are nearly absent — market proof replaces credential proof.
5. Strongest headline in the segment names the CEO's Tuesday, not their dreams: "Make Better CEO Decisions Under Pressure" (The CEO Project). Weakest are generic aspiration: "Be The Leader You Want To Be."
6. Avoid: unverifiable superlatives ("The #1 CEO Coach"), audience creep, aspiration wallpaper.
